\begin{frame}[allowframebreaks,t]{Summary}
  \begin{itemize}\scriptsize\itemsep0.28em
    \item[\textcolor{red}{$\blacktriangleright$}] \textbf{A long context costs compute, memory and quality.} Attention grows as $2n^2d$ FLOPs per layer and overtakes the weight matrices beyond $n = P/d$ (53K tokens for Llama~3.1~8B); the KV cache grows linearly, 128\,KiB per token and 16\,GiB per 128K-token request for the same model.
    \item[\textcolor{red}{$\blacktriangleright$}] \textbf{The usable window is shorter than the claimed one.} Lost in the middle gives a U-shaped accuracy curve over the position of the evidence; needle tests are weak evidence; RULER, BABILong and NoLiMa find effective lengths far below the advertised ones. A few billion tokens of continued pre-training extend the window, but using it is the hard part.
    \item[\textcolor{red}{$\blacktriangleright$}] \textbf{Prompt compression shrinks the input.} Hard methods delete tokens and work with any API (LLMLingua, $20\times$ on a GSM8K prompt; LongLLMLingua adds the question; LLMLingua-2 classifies tokens). Soft methods turn the prompt into a few vectors (gist tokens, ICAE, AutoCompressors) and need the model's weights. Exact strings are the first casualty.
    \item[\textcolor{red}{$\blacktriangleright$}] \textbf{KV-cache eviction keeps $B$ of the $n$ cached tokens.} Attention sinks plus a recent window bound memory without extending recall; H\textsubscript{2}O keeps heavy hitters, SnapKV lets the end of the prompt vote, PyramidKV gives lower layers more budget. Eviction fails when the question arrives after the decision or the context is reused.
    \item[\textcolor{red}{$\blacktriangleright$}] \textbf{Hierarchical attention reads near tokens finely and far tokens coarsely.} HAN pools words into sentences and sentences into documents; Longformer and BigBird combine local windows with global (and random) tokens at linear cost; H-Transformer-1D attends to distant blocks at coarser resolution. Fixed patterns choose positions without looking at the content.
    \item[\textcolor{red}{$\blacktriangleright$}] \textbf{Block-sparse attention lets the query choose.} Scoring block summaries and reading the top-$k$ blocks costs about $n/b + kb$ keys per query. Landmark tokens, MoBA, NSA and DeepSeek Sparse Attention differ in the unit selected and the selection signal; NSA and DSA are trained with their sparsity: NSA matches or beats its full-attention twin, and DSA cuts the 128K decode cost from \$2.15 to \$0.25 per million tokens.
    \item[\textcolor{red}{$\blacktriangleright$}] \textbf{Segment recurrence carries a memory across segments.} Transformer-XL reuses the previous segment's states (reach about $L \cdot s$, relative positions); the Compressive Transformer compresses old states; Memorizing Transformers retrieve past keys by kNN; the RMT trains a few memory tokens through time and reaches millions of tokens on simple retrieval.
    \item[\textcolor{red}{$\blacktriangleright$}] \textbf{Linear attention turns memory into a fixed-size state.} The state $S_t$ is an associative memory: additive writes interfere, the delta rule writes only the error, and Gated DeltaNet adds a decay. Infini-attention and Titans attach such memories to local attention. Fixed memories lose recall, so the 2025 hybrids keep one full-attention layer in four or eight.
    \item[\textcolor{red}{$\blacktriangleright$}] \textbf{Choose by measurement.} Frontier long-context models beat retrieval on average, but retrieval costs a fraction; routing between them (Self-Route) keeps most of the quality at 38--61\% of the long-context cost. Measure effective length on your own data first, and re-measure after every compression or sparsity change.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
