\begin{frame}{Limitations and Open Problems}
  \begin{itemize}\footnotesize\itemsep0.45em
    \item \textbf{Generated detail is not evidence.} Diffusion restorers sample one plausible image; invented texture, faces, vessels or field edges often pass PSNR, SSIM and no-reference scores. Hallucination metrics (an MLLM-based score, OpenSR-test's per-pixel labels) are new and not yet standard.
    \item \textbf{Benchmarks saturate or mislead.} Leading anomaly detectors often differ by less than a point on MVTec AD yet reach at most 58.7\% AU-PRO on MVTec AD 2; open-loop nuScenes scores reward ego status; on PANGAEA, the geospatial foundation models it tests do not consistently beat a U-Net; reported architecture gains in 3D segmentation shrink against a well-configured nnU-Net.
    \item \textbf{Shift breaks deployed models.} Synthetic noise and blur differ from real sensors. In the cases above, a new MRI vendor's data format made many reconstruction models useless, a lighting change halved one anomaly detector's AU-PRO, and at night a camera-only BEV model falls further behind LiDAR.
    \item \textbf{Compute and access.} SUPIR trained for 10 days on 64 GPUs and needs 12\,GB plus 16\,GB at inference; one-step distillation cuts inference cost, while the prior still has to be trained in full. Many strong checkpoints (SUPIR, FLUX.1 Fill [dev], InvSR, nnInteractive, ProPainter) are licensed for non-commercial use only, and MedSAM2's model card limits its weights to research and education.
    \item \textbf{Data and privacy.} Medical and roadside data identify people. Engineering practice: process on the device where possible, hash licence plates when only a match is needed, keep retention short, and validate on external sites before use.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
