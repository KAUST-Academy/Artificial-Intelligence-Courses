\section{Motivation for Policy Gradient Methods}
\begin{frame}{Motivation for Policy Gradient Methods}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Recall from RL Foundations: RL methods are either \textbf{value-based} or \textbf{policy-based}.
        \item So far we have covered the value-based branch: Q-learning, DQN, SARSA.
        \item Today we move to the other branch: \textbf{directly optimizing the policy}.
        \pause
        \item Value-based methods learn Q-values and derive the policy indirectly.
        \begin{itemize}
            \item Inefficient in continuous or large action spaces
            \item Can’t represent stochastic policies
            \item May lead to high variance and instability
        \end{itemize}
        \vspace{1em}
        \item[]\textbf{Policy Gradient Methods:}
        \begin{itemize}
            \item Learn policy parameters directly to maximize expected return:
            \[
                \mathcal{J}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\pi_\theta}\!\left[\sum_{t \geq 0} \gamma^t r_t\right]
            \]
        \end{itemize}
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
