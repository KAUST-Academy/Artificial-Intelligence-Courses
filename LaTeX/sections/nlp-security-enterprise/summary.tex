\begin{frame}{Summary: Security and Robustness}
  \begin{itemize}\scriptsize\setlength{\itemsep}{0.55em}\setlength{\parsep}{0pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item[\textcolor{red}{$\blacktriangleright$}] \textbf{Hallucination follows from how models are trained and graded.} Generating valid text is at least as hard as classifying it, the hallucination rate on arbitrary facts is at least the share of facts seen exactly once in training, and binary grading rewards guessing. Detect it without a reference by sampling (semantic entropy: AUROC 0.790 against 0.691 for string entropy), reduce it with verification (CoVe: FActScore 55.9 to 71.4) and attributed generation, and let the model abstain below a stated confidence target $t$.
    \item[\textcolor{red}{$\blacktriangleright$}] \textbf{Attacks on text are searches over tokens.} HotFlip scores every token flip with one gradient; GCG optimises a 20-token suffix toward ``Sure, here is'' and transfers to closed models (GPT-3.5: 86.6\%); PAIR and many-shot prompts need no gradients at all. Safety training fails through competing objectives and mismatched generalization; refusal is one direction in activation space, and ten fine-tuning examples for under \$0.20 remove it.
    \item[\textcolor{red}{$\blacktriangleright$}] \textbf{No single defense survives an adaptive attacker.} Perplexity filters and SmoothLLM stop gibberish suffixes but not fluent attacks; safety classifiers screen inputs and streamed outputs (0.38\% more refusals, 23.7\% more compute); circuit breakers cut mean ASR from 76.7\% to 9.8\% on standard attacks, yet adaptive attacks broke most of 12 published defenses above 90\%. Layer them, test with the strongest attack you can build, and pair every safety test with an over-refusal test.
    \item[\textcolor{red}{$\blacktriangleright$}] \textbf{A fixed number of poisoned documents is enough.} 250 documents backdoored models from 600M to 13B parameters; \$60 of expired domains could have controlled 0.01\% of LAION-400M; 100 correctly labelled examples poison instruction tuning; backdoors survived SFT, RLHF and adversarial training. Defend with provenance and hashes, small reviewed fine-tuning sets, trigger tests before release, and controlled writes to retrieval corpora.
    \item[\textcolor{red}{$\blacktriangleright$}] \textbf{Models memorize, and attacks read it back.} Extraction grows log-linearly with model size, duplication and context length; \$200 of queries recovered over 10,000 verbatim training examples from ChatGPT. Membership inference barely beats chance on LLM pretraining data. DP-SGD (clip each gradient, add Gaussian noise, account for every step) gives a formal $(\varepsilon,\delta)$ bound at a utility cost; deduplication, PII scrubbing, unlearning and redaction are partial controls.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Summary: Enterprise Architectures}
  \begin{itemize}\scriptsize\setlength{\itemsep}{0.4em}\setlength{\parsep}{0pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item[\textcolor{red}{$\blacktriangleright$}] \textbf{Use lexical and dense retrieval together, then rerank.} BM25 wins on exact identifiers and out of domain; bi-encoders handle paraphrase (DPR top-20: 78.4 against 59.1 on NQ); cross-encoders rerank a shortlist (11\% above BM25 on BEIR); ColBERT's MaxSim sits between. Fuse rankings with RRF ($k = 60$).
    \item[\textcolor{red}{$\blacktriangleright$}] \textbf{The pipeline decides what the answer can see.} Chunk on document structure (53.2\% against 48.2\% accuracy), add chunk context before indexing (failed retrievals 5.7\% to 1.9\% with reranking), filter by ACL inside retrieval, propagate deletes and permission changes, and key every cache on permissions.
    \item[\textcolor{red}{$\blacktriangleright$}] \textbf{Graphs answer what top-$k$ retrieval cannot.} GraphRAG's community summaries answer corpus-wide questions (preferred in 72--79\% of comprehensiveness comparisons); Personalized PageRank retrieves across hops (HippoRAG); text-to-query gives exact counts but needs schema checks and read-only credentials.
    \item[\textcolor{red}{$\blacktriangleright$}] \textbf{Parse layout before you retrieve.} 2-D positions lifted FUNSD F1 from 60.26 to 78.66 (LayoutLM); OCR-free and VLM parsers read pages end to end but can invent text; modular pipelines keep each element's page and box for citations.
    \item[\textcolor{red}{$\blacktriangleright$}] \textbf{A copilot proposes and the person decides; measure whether it helps.} 15\% more issues resolved per hour in customer support and 26\% more completed tasks for developers, yet experienced developers on familiar code took 19\% longer while believing they were faster. A copilot inherits every permission; one email sufficed for zero-click exfiltration (EchoLeak).
    \item[\textcolor{red}{$\blacktriangleright$}] \textbf{Climb the adaptation ladder one measured rung at a time:} prompting and RAG, then SFT or LoRA, then continued pretraining with replay and vocabulary extension; RAFT for domain RAG. A prompted generalist can beat a specialist (Medprompt: 90.2\% against 86.5\% on MedQA), so re-check with each new model.
    \item[\textcolor{red}{$\blacktriangleright$}] \textbf{Run the model yourself when data must stay inside, and size it:} break-even volume $V^{*} = 10^6 F / p$; open-weight models trailed closed ones by about four months in early 2026; a gateway enforces policy; S-LoRA serves 2,000 adapters on one base model; 4-bit weights fit a 70B model on one 80\,GB GPU (about 13 concurrent 8k sessions).
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
