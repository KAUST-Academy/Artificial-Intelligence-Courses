\begin{frame}{Learning Outcomes}
  By the end of this lecture you should be able to:
  \begin{itemize}\footnotesize\itemsep0.12em\setlength{\parskip}{0.2em}
    \item[\textcolor{red}{$\blacktriangleright$}] \textbf{Explain} why language models hallucinate, and \textbf{detect and reduce} hallucination with sampling checks, verification, citations, and abstention.
    \item[\textcolor{red}{$\blacktriangleright$}] \textbf{Construct} adversarial attacks and jailbreaks, from token flips and universal triggers to GCG, PAIR, and many-shot prompts, and \textbf{explain} why safety training fails.
    \item[\textcolor{red}{$\blacktriangleright$}] \textbf{Choose} layered jailbreak defenses and \textbf{evaluate} them against adaptive attacks and over-refusal.
    \item[\textcolor{red}{$\blacktriangleright$}] \textbf{Analyse} how poisoned data plants backdoors, and \textbf{quantify} privacy leakage through memorization, extraction, and membership inference.
    \item[\textcolor{red}{$\blacktriangleright$}] \textbf{Design} an enterprise retrieval stack: BM25, dense and late-interaction retrievers, fusion, reranking, and vector search at scale.
    \item[\textcolor{red}{$\blacktriangleright$}] \textbf{Design} a production RAG system with contextual chunking, permission-aware retrieval, knowledge graphs, and parsed documents.
    \item[\textcolor{red}{$\blacktriangleright$}] \textbf{Decide} how to build and run an enterprise assistant: the copilot architecture, a domain-adaptation route, and an on-premises serving stack.
  \end{itemize}
  \vspace{0.1em}
  \begin{itemize}\scriptsize
    \item[\textcolor{red}{$\blacktriangleright$}] \textbf{Prerequisite knowledge:} BERT, LoRA; LLM evaluation; prompt injection, agent safeguards, governance; RAG, contrastive learning, ANN indexes; serving, quantization, multi-GPU inference; ViT, detection.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
